About

Trust should be re-runnable.

Ellery is named after a detective. It treats a dataset like a case: evidence in, findings recorded, a verdict you can reopen and argue from the ground up.

The idea

Most analytics tools ask you to make the chart and trust the person who made it. Ellery inverts that. The unit of value is a claim — a statement with evidence — not a chart. Charts, dashboards, and slides are just renderings of claims.

Because the reasoning is deterministic, trust can detach from the author and attach to a re-runnable procedure — the way it does in accounting, in a mathematical proof, or in a reproducible experiment. The same data under the same policy always yields the same findings, and any finding can be pulled apart to the exact cells that produced it.

Principles

  • One reasoning brain. Conclusions come from one deterministic engine; the interface displays truth, it never manufactures it.
  • Evidence flows forward. Every layer reads only the one beneath it, down to fingerprinted cells.
  • No model in the truth path. Models may propose; only deterministic code may assert.
  • Say nothing proudly. When the data is calm, Ellery says so instead of inventing a story.
  • One excellent thing. One memorable interaction over ten mediocre features.

Who's building it
